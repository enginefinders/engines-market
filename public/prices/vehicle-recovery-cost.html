⚠️ Important Recovery Safety: Professional recovery uses a flatbed truck — your vehicle is loaded onto the flatbed and transported. This is the safest method and prevents damage to the transmission (especially critical for automatic and 4WD vehicles). Never accept a tow rope recovery for a vehicle that can't be driven — towing can cause catastrophic transmission damage.

Frequently asked questions

Common Questions About Vehicle Recovery

Everything you need to know about recovery rates, dispatch, and combined workshop fitting.

How much does vehicle recovery cost in the UK? +
Vehicle recovery costs range from £90 to £300+ depending on distance. Local recovery (≤10 miles) costs £90-£165, regional recovery (10-50 miles) costs £120-£250, and national recovery (50+ miles) costs £150-£300+. The call-out fee is typically £50-£150 plus a mileage rate of £1.50-£2.00 per mile.
What's included in vehicle recovery? +
Call-out, flatbed recovery (your vehicle is loaded onto a flatbed truck), and transport to your chosen workshop. Basic winching may also be included. Out-of-hours calls and difficult access may incur extra charges.
Do I have breakdown cover already? +
Many breakdown policies (AA, RAC, Green Flag) include basic recovery. Some insurance policies include breakdown cover. Premium bank accounts may include breakdown cover. Vehicle warranties may include breakdown cover. Check your policy documents before paying for recovery.
How long will I wait for recovery? +
Urban areas: 1-2 hours. City centres: 2-4 hours. Rural areas: 2-6 hours. Scottish Highlands: 4-24+ hours. Peak times (weekends, bank holidays, bad weather) add 2-4 hours.
What's the difference between flatbed recovery and towing? +
Flatbed recovery loads your vehicle onto a flatbed truck - safe for all vehicles. Towing (tow rope or tow dolly) can damage the transmission, especially on automatic and 4WD vehicles. Professional recovery always uses a flatbed.
Can you recover my vehicle to a workshop that does engine replacement? +
Yes - we offer a combined recovery + engine replacement service. Your vehicle is recovered to a specialist workshop, and the engine is replaced at the same location. This is often cheaper and simpler than arranging recovery and replacement separately.
Do I need to be with the vehicle for recovery? +
Yes - you should stay with the vehicle until the recovery vehicle arrives. You may travel with the recovery driver to your chosen workshop. If you can't stay with the vehicle, you'll need to arrange for someone else to be there.
What if my vehicle is in a difficult position? +
If your vehicle is in a difficult position (e.g., ditch, off-road), a specialist recovery vehicle may be needed. This will cost more and take longer. The recovery provider will advise.
Does recovery cover Scotland and remote areas? +
Yes - we cover all regions of the UK, including Scotland. However, remote areas (Scottish Highlands, islands) may have surcharges or longer wait times. Recovery in these areas is typically arranged locally.
What if I need my vehicle recovered out of hours? +
Out-of-hours recovery (evening, weekend, bank holiday) typically costs £50-£100 extra. Recovery providers operate 24/7, but out-of-hours calls are more expensive.
Can I choose which workshop my vehicle is taken to? +
Yes - you choose the workshop. We can recommend specialists if you don't have a preferred workshop. The recovery driver will take the vehicle to your chosen destination.
What if my vehicle can't be loaded onto a flatbed? +
If your vehicle is in a difficult position, specialist recovery may be needed. The recovery provider will assess the situation and advise. This will cost more.
Is recovery covered by insurance? +
Some insurance policies include breakdown cover - check your policy documents. If not, you'll need to pay for recovery yourself. Many premium policies include breakdown cover as standard.
Do I need to tell my insurer about recovery? +
If you're claiming on insurance for the breakdown (e.g., accident damage), you'll need to inform your insurer. If it's a mechanical breakdown and you're not claiming, you don't need to tell your insurer - but you may need to if you're making a claim.
