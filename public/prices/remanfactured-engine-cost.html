Remanufactured Engine Cost Summary

What does a remanufactured engine cost in the UK?

The short answer: A remanufactured engine typically costs £3,000–£7,000+ depending on engine size and application. This is 40% less than a new OE unit while delivering equivalent performance and reliability. Fitting adds £800–£2,200 depending on engine type and region.

The longer answer: For most UK drivers replacing a common 4-cylinder engine with a remanufactured unit, expect to pay between £3,000 and £5,000 for the engine supply. Premium 6-cylinder and V8 remanufactured engines typically cost £5,000–£7,000+. Commercial vehicle remanufactured engines for vans and light trucks range from £4,000–£8,000+.

Based on 24,650 real quote requests processed through Engines Market in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], remanufactured engines represent approximately 2–3% of total replacement requests — but this is growing rapidly as commercial fleets, insurer networks, and warranty companies recognise the value proposition.

What "Remanufactured" Actually Means

The gold standard of engine restoration

A remanufactured engine is the highest-quality engine restoration available. Unlike a reconditioned or rebuilt engine, a remanufactured engine is returned to manufacturer's original specification using a combination of new parts, remanufactured core components, and rigorous dyno/bench testing.

🛡️
12-Mo Warranty

BS AU257:2002 Code of Practice for the Remanufacture of Automotive Engines
  • Provide performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine.
  • Be returned to manufacturer's original specification and tolerances.
  • Use brand new parts for all critical wearable components.
  • Pass rigorous bench testing to verify mechanical performance.
  • Be supplied with a comprehensive written warranty.

[THIRD-PARTY] British Standards Institution standard for automotive remanufacture.

Important: BS AU257:2002 is the industry benchmark for quality. Any engine described as "remanufactured" should meet or exceed this standard. Always ask your supplier if they are BS AU257:2002 certified.

Procurement Methods

Core exchange vs sending your engine

Most remanufacturers offer core exchange as the default fast option, while custom build-to-order preserves original vehicle matching numbers.

🔄 Core Exchange (Recommended)

You send your old engine as an exchange "core" and receive a pre-remanufactured engine immediately off the shelf.

⏱️ Timeline: 5–10 days 💰 Cost: 10–15% lower ✅ Pros: Fast, off-shelf availability ❌ Cons: Lose matching serials
Best for: Commercial fleets, insurance repairs, daily drivers.
📦 Send Your Engine (Custom Rebuild)

You remove your original engine and courier it to the remanufacturer. They remanufacture and dyno-test your exact engine block and return it.

⏱️ Timeline: 3–6 weeks 💰 Cost: Full custom build cost ✅ Pros: Retains matching numbers ❌ Cons: Longer garage downtime
Best for: Classic cars, collector vehicles, matching-numbers restorations.

Warranty Coverage

What's included in remanufactured engine warranty

The industry standard for remanufactured engines is a 12-month unlimited mileage parts and labour warranty. This is significantly higher than reconditioned engines and equivalent to many new OEM warranties.

✅ What the warranty covers

  • Parts: All replacement components (pistons, rings, bearings, timing components, gaskets, seals).
  • Labour: Garage labour costs to remove and refit if the engine fails under warranty.
  • Machining defects: Flaws in block line-boring, crank regrinding, or head skimming.
  • Assembly defects: Any failure attributable to workshop assembly errors.
  • Unlimited mileage: No mileage cap during the 12-month coverage period.

❌ What the warranty typically excludes

⚠️ Customer-Induced Damage
Running without oil, severe overheating, or continuous redline over-revving.
⚙️ Ancillary Failures
External alternator, starter motor, turbo, or injectors unless supplied with the build.
🔧 Installation Errors
Incorrect timing setup or inadequate oil priming by an uncertified fitter.
🔩 Post-Warranty Wear
Normal high-mileage wear and tear after the expiration of the 12-month term.

Buyer's Due Diligence

10 questions to ask your remanufacturer

1. "Are you BS AU257:2002 certified?"
Single most important question. If they aren't certified, they cannot legitimately market units as remanufactured.
2. "What is included in the remanufacturing process?"
Request written confirmation of full machining, brand new wear parts, and pressure testing standards.
3. "What warranty do you offer and what does it cover?"
Industry benchmark is 12-month unlimited mileage parts and labour. Confirm labour reimbursement rates.
4. "Do you offer core exchange or do I send my engine?"
Confirm whether your core condition (e.g., holed block vs seized) is acceptable for full surcharge refund.
5. "Can you provide BS AU257:2002 certification documentation?"
A bona fide remanufacturer will provide ISO / BSI certificates upon request.
6. "What brand new parts are fitted as standard?"
Pistons, rings, main bearings, big ends, thrust washers, timing kits, and all gaskets must be 100% new.
7. "What testing is performed before dispatch?"
Confirm live compression testing, oil pressure verification, and leak-down testing results.
8. "Can I see photos of previous remanufactured builds?"
Look for clinical clean-room assembly environments and precision packaging.
9. "What is the guaranteed delivery schedule?"
Core exchange units should arrive within 5–10 days. Ensure courier pallet tracking is included.
10. "Can I check verified commercial client reviews?"
Verify fleet manager feedback on Trustpilot, Google, and trade associations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about remanufactured engines

What's the difference between remanufactured and reconditioned engines? +
A remanufactured engine is returned to manufacturer's specification with new parts throughout and must meet BS AU257:2002 Code of Practice. It provides performance and reliability equivalent to the original engine. A reconditioned engine is cleaned and has only worn parts replaced without adhering to formal British Standards. Remanufactured engines cost 30–40% more but offer significantly higher quality and longevity.
How much does a remanufactured engine cost in the UK? +
Remanufactured engine costs range from £3,000 to £7,000+ depending on engine size and application. Small 4-cylinder engines cost £3,000–£4,000; 6-cylinder engines cost £4,500–£6,500; V8 and commercial engines cost £5,500–£8,500+. Fitting adds £800–£2,200. Prices are ex VAT and assume core exchange.
Is a remanufactured engine as good as new? +
Yes. BS AU257:2002 mandates that a remanufactured engine must provide performance, tolerances, and reliability equivalent to the original engine. With new wear parts throughout and comprehensive testing, it delivers like-new performance at 40% less than a new OE unit.
What's BS AU257:2002? +
BS AU257:2002 is the British Standard Code of Practice for the Remanufacture of Automotive Engines. It specifies strict engineering rules: returning parts to factory dimensions, fitting new pistons/bearings/chains, and dyno-testing. Always ask your supplier if they are BS AU257:2002 certified.
Why is remanufactured cheaper than new? +
Remanufactured engines cost 40% less than new OE units because: the engine block, crankshaft, and cylinder head castings are precision remanufactured rather than newly mined and cast; batch processing yields high economies of scale; and there are no franchised dealer markups.
What warranty comes with a remanufactured engine? +
Industry standard is a 12-month unlimited mileage parts and labour warranty. Some remanufacturers offer extended warranties (24 months) for an additional cost. The warranty covers machining defects, assembly defects, and component failures.
How long does a remanufactured engine last? +
A properly remanufactured engine meeting BS AU257:2002 should last 100,000+ miles if maintained correctly. The remanufacture includes new pistons, rings, bearings, timing components, and gaskets. Regular oil changes and servicing are essential for longevity.
What's core exchange? +
Core exchange is where you send your old engine as a "core" and receive a remanufactured engine that has already been prepared. It is faster (5–10 days), cheaper (10–15% discount), but you exchange your original serial number. It is the most popular option for fleets and insurance repairs.
Can I keep my original engine (matching numbers)? +
Yes, by choosing the "send your engine" custom rebuild route. Your exact engine is remanufactured and returned to you. This takes 3–6 weeks but preserves matching numbers for classic and high-value performance cars.
Who should choose a remanufactured engine? +
Commercial fleets (minimising van downtime), classic car restorers (matching original specifications), insurance/warranty claims (documented quality standards), and private owners with £25,000+ vehicles planning 5+ years of ownership.
What's the most popular engine for remanufacturing? +
Based on UK data: Ford Transit 2.0 EcoBlue (high fleet demand), BMW N47/N57 (timing chain failures), Land Rover TDV6/SDV6 (crankshaft bearing failure), Mercedes OM651/OM642, and Volkswagen/Audi 2.0 TDI. Classic car demand is led by Jaguar E-Type XK, MGB, and Mini A-Series.
Do I need to tell my insurer about a remanufactured engine? +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including a like-for-like remanufactured engine, should be declared to your insurance provider and updated on your DVLA V5C registration document if the engine serial number changes.
Is a remanufactured engine environmentally friendly? +
Yes. Remanufacturing uses significantly less energy and raw materials than smelting and casting a new engine from scratch. Reusing massive metal castings reduces industrial carbon emissions, embodying circular economy principles.
Can I upgrade components during remanufacturing? +
BS AU257:2002 remanufacturing focuses on returning an engine to factory specification. If you require forged pistons, uprated turbo internals, or high-lift camshafts, request a custom performance rebuild from the specialist.
