THE SHORT ANSWER

What does engine fitting cost in the UK?

Engine fitting costs typically range from £800 to £2,500 depending on vehicle complexity, engine type, and your location. Most standard front-wheel-drive vehicles cost £800-£1,500 to fit, while 4WD, performance, and prestige vehicles cost £1,500-£2,500+.

Labour accounts for 40-60% of total engine replacement expenses. For most UK drivers, the labour to remove an old engine and fit a replacement is:

Standard FWD: £800-£1,500 (8-12 hrs)
RWD vehicles: £1,000-£1,800 (10-15 hrs)
4WD/SUVs: £1,500-£2,500 (12-20 hrs)
Commercial vans: £1,000-£2,000 (8-14 hrs)
Prestige/Performance: £1,800-£3,500+ (16-24 hrs)

[EM-VERIFIED] based on 24,650 real quote requests. Average labour rate in the UK is currently £68.51 per hour [THIRD-PARTY].

WHAT'S INCLUDED

What you're paying for

When you pay for engine fitting, here's what's typically included in the standard labour charge:

🔧
Vehicle preparation
Lifting the vehicle, draining fluids, removing components for access.
🛠️
Engine removal
Disconnecting all ancillaries, removing the old engine cleanly.
🔄
Component transfer
Moving necessary ancillaries from old engine to replacement unit.
🔁
Engine installation
Fitting the replacement engine, reconnecting wiring, fuel & cooling systems.
💧
Fluid refill
Fresh high-grade oil, coolant, and other essential fluids.
📋
Basic testing
Compression test, oil pressure check, and leak-down verification.
🛣️
Test drive
Controlled road test to verify real-world operational performance.
♻️
Disposal of old unit
Environmentally compliant scrappage & recycling of your old engine core.

Always check the written quote for exact inclusions. Some garages include consumables (oil, filters) in the labour charge; others itemise them separately.

WHAT'S NOT INCLUDED

Common exclusions to watch for

When you provide your own engine for fitting, here's what's typically NOT covered in a labour-only quote:

🔗
Engine supply
You provide your own engine - the garage does not supply it.
🛡️
Warranty on customer engine
The garage does NOT warranty your engine - you accept the risk.
🔧
Clutch and DMF
Manual gearbox: clutch and DMF often worn. Extra £400-£1,000.
💧
Water pump / thermostat
Often recommended as a precaution. Add £100-£300.
❄️
Air-con regas
Removing engine requires disconnecting pipes. Regas extra £50-£150.
⏱️
Timing belt / chain
Some replacement engines supplied without. Add £300-£800.
🔍
Unforeseen repairs
Worn mounts, exhaust leaks, corroded pipes discovered during fitting.
🚗
Hire / courtesy car
Unless offered, you'll need alternative transport during fitting.
⚠️ Critical disclaimer:

The garage does NOT warranty your customer-supplied engine. If you source your own engine and it fails internally, the garage is not responsible. They have fitted the engine correctly - the mechanical risk of the unit itself is entirely yours. Ask the garage to perform a pre-installation check before fitting.

CUSTOMER-SUPPLIED ENGINE

Important considerations when supplying your own engine

If you've sourced your own engine (used, reconditioned, rebuilt, or remanufactured) and need it fitting, here are the key considerations.

✓ The Garage's Role

  • Remove your old engine
  • Fit your replacement engine
  • Transfer ancillaries
  • Refill fresh fluids
  • Test the physical installation

✗ The Garage's Role is NOT

  • Warranty your supplied engine unit
  • Guarantee the internal health of your engine
  • Cover labour if your replacement engine is faulty

Your responsibilities when you supply your own engine

⚠️ The risk that the engine may be faulty internally
⚠️ The risk that the engine may fail shortly after fitting
⚠️ The cost of removal and refitting if the engine fails
⚠️ The risk that the engine may not be compatible with your vehicle

Questions to ask the garage before fitting

1. Will you inspect my engine before fitting?

Ask if they can perform basic crank and visual checks before starting.

2. What happens if the engine is faulty?

Confirm the procedure and costs if an issue is discovered.

3. Can you verify the engine code matches my vehicle?

Ensure engine code parity before mechanical teardown begins.

4. What's your process for testing after fitting?

Check if compression, pressure, and road tests are carried out.

5. What warranty do you offer on the fitting work?

Ensure labour workmanship is backed by a 6-12 month warranty.

BEFORE YOU AGREE

10 questions to ask before fitting your customer-supplied engine

Asking the right questions can save you thousands and prevent disputes. Here's a checklist specific to fitting a customer-supplied engine.

1. Will you inspect my engine before fitting?

Some garages offer a pre-installation inspection (compression, oil pressure, visual). May cost £100-£300 but could save thousands.

2. What happens if the engine is faulty?

Clarify who bears the risk. The garage is not responsible. Get this in writing.

3. Can you verify the engine code matches my vehicle?

Engine codes must match exactly. Ask the garage to verify compatibility before fitting.

4. What's included in the fitting cost?

Get a full written breakdown: removal, fitting, consumables, disposal, testing.

5. What's NOT included that might cost extra?

Clutch/DMF, water pump, timing components, air-con regas, unforeseen repairs.

6. What warranty do you offer on the fitting work?

The fitting work itself should be warranted. Ask about the period and how to claim.

7. What's your timeline for fitting?

Get a realistic estimate. 4-cyl FWD: 2-3 days; V8 4WD: 4-5 days.

8. Do you have experience with my engine type?

Specialist knowledge matters - some engines require specialist tools and knowledge.

9. Can you provide a written quote?

Get everything in writing before agreeing: scope, cost, timeline, warranty terms.

10. What happens if you discover additional issues?

Worn mounts, exhaust leaks, corroded pipes. Are these quoted separately? Do you get approval first?

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Common questions about engine fitting costs

How much does engine fitting cost in the UK? +
Engine fitting costs typically range from £800 to £2,500 depending on vehicle complexity, engine type, and your location. Standard front-wheel-drive vehicles cost £800-£1,500; 4WD and performance vehicles cost £1,500-£2,500+. Labour accounts for 40-60% of total engine replacement expenses.
How long does engine fitting take? +
Typical fitting time is 2-5 days. Standard 4-cylinder FWD vehicles take 2-3 days. V8 4WD vehicles take 4-5 days. Complex or rare engines may take longer. The timeline depends on vehicle complexity, parts availability, and the garage's workload.
What's included in engine fitting? +
Removal of old engine, fitting of customer engine, transfer of ancillaries, new consumables (oil, coolant, filters), basic testing (compression, oil pressure), and disposal of old unit. Always check the written quote for exact inclusions.
What's NOT included in engine fitting? +
Engine supply (you provide your own engine), warranty on your customer-supplied engine, clutch/DMF, water pump, timing components, air-con regas, unforeseen repairs, and hire car. Your engine is not warranted - you accept the risk.
What's the labour rate for engine fitting in the UK? +
The average labour rate in the UK is currently £68.51 per hour [THIRD-PARTY]. Independent specialists charge £55-£160/hour (regional variation). Main dealers charge £90-£220/hour (regional variation). Independent specialists are typically 40-60% cheaper than main dealers.
Should I use an independent specialist or a main dealer? +
For most customers, an independent specialist offers the best balance of cost and quality. Main dealers are significantly more expensive (40-60% higher) but offer manufacturer training and specialist tools. Use a main dealer if your vehicle is under warranty or you're claiming on insurance.
Can I supply my own engine and have it fitted? +
Yes. Many garages are happy to fit customer-supplied engines. However, the garage does NOT warranty your engine - you accept the risk. Ask the garage to inspect your engine before fitting. Get everything in writing.
What happens if my customer-supplied engine is faulty? +
If you supply your own engine and it's faulty, the garage is not responsible. You'll pay for removal and refitting again. Ask the garage to inspect the engine before fitting to verify its condition. This may cost extra but could save you thousands.
How long does a fitted engine take to run in? +
Most engines require a run-in period of 500-1,000 miles. Avoid heavy acceleration, high revs, and towing during this period. Vary the engine speed (don't sit at the same speed on motorways). Follow the garage's specific run-in instructions.
Do I need to tell my insurer about the new engine? +
Yes. Any major mechanical change, including engine replacement, must be declared to your insurer. The new engine code and number should be recorded on your V5C (logbook) and insurance policy. Failure to do so could invalidate your insurance.
What's the difference between engine fitting and engine replacement? +
Engine replacement typically includes supply of a new or reconditioned engine plus fitting. Engine fitting is labour-only - you supply the engine and the garage fits it. If you've already sourced your own engine, you need engine fitting.
Can I watch the engine being fitted? +
Some garages allow customers to observe the work, others don't for insurance and safety reasons. Ask the garage about their policy. Trusted garages will provide photos and updates during the process.
What testing is included in engine fitting? +
Basic testing is typically included: compression test, oil pressure test, visual inspection for leaks, and a short test drive. Comprehensive dyno testing is usually extra (£100-£300) and only available at some garages.
Should I replace ancillaries during engine fitting? +
If your alternator, starter, injectors, turbo, or other ancillaries are worn, it's sensible to replace them while the engine is out. The labour cost to replace them later is much higher. Ask the garage to inspect your ancillaries and advise.
