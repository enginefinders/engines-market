Used engine cost summary

What does a used engine cost in the UK?

The short answer: Used engines cost between £500 and £3,200 supply-only. Fitted costs range from £1,000 to £4,500 depending on engine type, mileage, and condition.

The longer answer: For most UK drivers replacing a common 4-cylinder diesel or petrol engine, expect to pay £900-£2,200 supply-only for a used unit with reasonable mileage (60,000-80,000 miles). Fitting adds £800-£2,200 depending on your location and the garage you choose.

Understanding used engines

What does "used engine" actually mean?

Understanding what a "used engine" actually is - and what it isn't - is critical before you buy one.

Typical Mileage Range

Used engines typically come from donor vehicles with 40,000 to 120,000 miles. The mileage is usually known from the donor vehicle's odometer, but in some cases, it may be estimated.

Mileage BandTypical ConditionTypical Price Impact
Under 40,000 milesLow mileage, likely well-maintainedPremium (+20-30%)
40,000-80,000 milesAverage, reasonable conditionStandard
80,000-120,000 milesHigher mileage, more wearDiscounted (-10-20%)
Over 120,000 milesHigh mileage, significant wearLowest price

Important: Mileage claims are not always verified. Some suppliers may not be able to confirm the exact mileage of a used engine, especially if it came from a written-off vehicle with a damaged dashboard or cluster.

Tested vs Untested - What's the difference?

✅ Tested Engine
  • The engine has been run on a test stand or in a vehicle
  • Compression test has been performed on all cylinders
  • Visual inspection has been carried out
  • Sometimes a video of the engine running is available
  • Usually carries a short warranty (30 days to 3 months)
⚠️ Untested Engine
  • Removed from a donor vehicle but not tested
  • May or may not run
  • No compression test or running video available
  • Usually sold "as-is" with no warranty or very limited warranty
  • Cheaper, but significantly higher risk

Used engine warranty

What's covered - and what's not

Warranty terms for used engines vary significantly. Here's what to expect.

Common Warranty Durations

DurationTypical CoverageCommon For
7 daysParts only, return to supplierUntested engines
30 daysParts onlyCheap used engines
3 monthsParts only or parts + labourMost used engines
6 monthsParts + labour (limited)Premium used engines

✅ What Warranty Typically Covers

  • Internal engine components: Pistons, rings, bearings, camshaft, crankshaft, connecting rods
  • Cylinder head components: Valves, springs, camshafts (if included)
  • Structural integrity: Block cracks (if discovered early)
  • Oil pressure: Confirmed within specification

❌ What Warranty Typically Does NOT Cover

💷 Labour
Most used engine warranties are parts-only. If the engine fails, you pay the fitting cost again.
🔧 Incorrect installation
If the engine is fitted incorrectly, the warranty is void.
⚠️ Neglect
Running with no oil, overheating, or ignoring warning lights voids the warranty.
🏁 Racing or track use
Used engines are not warranted for performance use.
⚙️ Ancillary components
Alternator, starter, injectors, turbo, pumps are usually not covered.
🔍 Unforeseen damage
If fitting reveals additional issues (damaged wiring, broken mounts), these are not covered.
🔩 Wear and tear
Normal wear components (seals, gaskets, belts) are not covered.
The Labour Coverage Problem: This is the biggest hidden cost of used engine warranties.

Scenario: You buy a used engine for £1,200 with a 3-month parts-only warranty. After 6 weeks, it develops a knocking noise. The supplier agrees to replace the engine under warranty.

What's covered: The replacement engine (if they have one in stock).
What's NOT covered: The £800-£1,500 labour cost to remove the failed engine and fit the replacement.
Result: You're out of pocket for the labour, and the car is off the road again.
Our advice: Ask every supplier: "Is your warranty parts-only or parts AND labour?" Get the answer in writing.

Risks of buying a used engine

Be brutally honest about the risks

A used engine is cheap for a reason. Here's what you need to know before buying.

📋 1. Unknown Service History

Most used engines come with no documented service history. You have no way of knowing how often the oil was changed, whether the correct oil was used, or if the engine was overheated.

🔩 2. No Upgraded Components

A used engine uses the original parts it came with - old seals, gaskets, timing components, piston rings, and bearings that may be at end of life.

🔍 3. Potential Hidden Damage

Hairline cracks in the cylinder head, block porosity, borescope damage, internal wear from oil starvation - these may not show up until the engine is fitted.

💷 4. No Labour Warranty (The Hidden Cost)

Even with a parts warranty, you'll almost certainly have to pay for labour again if the engine fails - adding £800-£2,200 to the cost.

📊 5. Mileage May Be Inaccurate

Odometer may have been damaged in the accident that wrote off the vehicle, or the cluster may have been swapped. You could be paying a premium for higher mileage.

⚙️ 6. Ancillary Components Are Still Old

Alternator, starter, injectors, turbo, pumps - if they fail shortly after fitting, you'll pay labour again.

🔌 7. Compatibility Issues

Different ECU calibrations, emissions equipment, flywheel/clutch configurations, and sensor packages can cause problems even with the same engine code.

🧪 8. No Testing / Poor Testing

"Tested" can mean different things - a compression test (good), a visual inspection (minimal), a running test (best), or just "we think it works" (dangerous).

Risk Summary Checklist

Before buying any used engine, run through this checklist:

✓ Is the mileage verified?
✓ Does it have documented service history?
✓ Has it been compression tested?
✓ Is there a video of it running?
✓ What does the warranty actually cover?
✓ Is labour covered by the warranty?
✓ Who fits it and what warranty do they offer?
✓ Is the engine code an exact match?
✓ Has it been pressure tested?
✓ Can you get the engine inspected before fitting?
If you can't answer "yes" to at least 6 of these, consider whether the risk is worth the saving.

How to inspect a used engine before buying

8 steps to protect yourself

If you're buying a used engine, here's what you should check before handing over any money.

🔢 1. Verify the Engine Code

The engine code must match your vehicle's VIN. Even small variations (N47D20A vs N47D20C) can cause compatibility issues. Ask the supplier to confirm and cross-reference it with your VIN.

📊 2. Ask for Mileage and Documentation

Ask for documented mileage from the donor vehicle. If the supplier can't provide it, be cautious. "What's the verified mileage of this engine? Do you have documentation?"

📋 3. Request a Compression Test Report

A compression test tells you the condition of the piston rings and valves. Ask for a report showing compression values for each cylinder and variation (should be within 10%).

🎥 4. Ask for a Running Video

Listen for unusual noises (knocking, ticking, rattling), excessive smoke (blue = oil, white = coolant, black = fuel), and smooth idle. "Can you send a video of this engine running?"

🔍 5. Inspect for Visible Damage

Check for oil leaks (main seals, valve cover, sump), coolant leaks, cracked/damaged mounting points, signs of overheating, and missing/broken ancillaries.

🛡️ 6. Check the Warranty Terms

Get the warranty terms in writing: duration, coverage (parts-only or parts + labour), exclusions, and claims process. "Can you send me your warranty terms in writing before I buy?"

🏢 7. Verify the Supplier

Check the supplier is legitimate: UK registered company (Companies House number), physical business address, online reviews, and at least 12 months trading history.

⚙️ 8. Ask About Ancillaries

Check what's included: alternator, starter motor, injectors, turbo, wiring loom, ECU. "What ancillaries are included with this engine?"

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about used engines

How much does a used engine cost in the UK? +
Used engines cost £500-£3,200 supply-only depending on engine type, mileage, and condition. Fitted costs range from £1,000 to £4,500. Four-cylinder petrol engines are cheapest (£500-£1,500), V8 and high-performance engines are most expensive (£1,800-£3,500+).
Is a used engine a good idea? +
A used engine is a good idea if your vehicle is worth under £5,000 and you plan to keep it for less than 12-18 months. If your vehicle is worth more than £5,000 or you plan to keep it longer, a reconditioned engine is usually better value long-term.
What's the difference between tested and untested? +
A tested engine has been compression tested and inspected. It may have a video of it running. An untested engine has not been checked and is sold "as-is." Tested engines cost more but carry lower risk.
What warranty comes with a used engine? +
Typically 1-6 months. Most used engine warranties are parts-only (you pay labour if it fails). Some suppliers offer parts and labour warranties for premium used engines. Always check in writing.
Can I fit a used engine from a different vehicle? +
Yes, but you need the exact same engine code and often the same variant to ensure compatibility. Your supplier will match the engine code to your vehicle's VIN. Never assume a similar engine will fit.
What happens if the used engine fails after fitting? +
If you have a parts warranty, the supplier will replace the engine (if available). You will almost certainly need to pay the labour cost again to remove the failed unit and fit the replacement. This is usually £800-£2,200.
How do I know if a used engine has been properly tested? +
Ask for a compression test report. Ask for a video of the engine running. Listen for unusual noises. If the supplier can't provide these, assume the engine hasn't been properly tested.
Is a used engine cheaper than a reconditioned engine? +
Yes. Used engines are typically £1,500-£3,000 cheaper than reconditioned engines. However, the lower price comes with higher risk and shorter warranty.
What's the best mileage for a used engine? +
Ideally under 80,000 miles for diesel and under 60,000 miles for petrol. However, condition and maintenance are more important than mileage alone. A well-maintained 80,000-mile engine is better than a neglected 40,000-mile engine.
Should I buy a used engine with high mileage? +
Only if the price is significantly lower and you're willing to accept the risk. High-mileage engines (over 100,000 miles) are more likely to have worn components and hidden issues.
Can I negotiate the price of a used engine? +
Yes. Used engines are often negotiable, especially if the supplier has had them in stock for a while. Ask if there's any flexibility on the price, especially if you're buying a used engine without ancillaries.

What vehicles are used engines typically bought for? +
96% of used engine requests are for vehicles over 15 years old or valued at £5,000 or less [EM-VERIFIED]. Used engines are predominantly chosen for older, lower-value vehicles.
What should I do if the used engine fails? +
Contact the supplier immediately. Follow their warranty claims process. Keep all documentation (receipt, warranty terms, communication). Be prepared to pay labour costs again if your warranty is parts-only.
Can I buy a used engine online? +
Yes. Most used engine suppliers operate online. However, we recommend doing thorough due diligence: check reviews, verify the supplier's business registration, ask for videos and photos, and get warranty terms in writing before paying.
