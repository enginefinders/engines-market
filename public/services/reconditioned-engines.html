What Is a Reconditioned Engine?

A reconditioned engine is a used engine that has been fully dismantled, cleaned, inspected, machined where necessary, and rebuilt with new wearing parts. It is not simply a "cleaned up" used engine - it is a professionally rebuilt unit with documented work and a warranty.

What's Included in a Typical Reconditioned Engine Quote

  • Engine unit (long block): Complete reconditioned block and cylinder head.
  • New internal wear parts: New piston rings, bearings, seals, gaskets replaced to serviceable standard.
  • Timing components: Chain/belt, tensioners, guides replaced.
  • Core labour: Labour for engine removal and refit (standard labour for engine swap).
  • Standard fluids: Oil, coolant, basic gaskets.
  • Disposal of old unit: Old engine collected and scrapped responsibly.
  • Warranty (6-12 months): Parts and labour on internal mechanical failure.

What's NOT Included (typically)

  • VAT: May be excluded from initial quoted price (always verify).
  • Ancillary replacements: Turbo, injectors, alternator, starter - extra if failed.
  • Clutch / DMF: If manual gearbox - extra £400-£1,000 if replacement needed.
  • Delivery charges: Some suppliers include; others charge £50-£200 for delivery.
  • Pallet return fees: £50-£150 if return transport of exchange unit is required.
  • Air-con regas: £50-£150 after engine refit discharge.
  • Unforeseen repairs: Seized bolts/broken studs - quoted separately.

Always ask your supplier for a full written breakdown before agreeing to any work. 'Reconditioned' does NOT mean factory-spec tolerances, OEM certification, or brand new components. The core block, cylinder head, and crankshaft are original (machined where necessary).

The Reconditioning Process

A proper reconditioning process takes time. Here's what happens at each stage:

01
Strip - Full Dismantling

Engine completely stripped: cylinder head, pistons, crankshaft, camshafts, timing components, oil pump, water pump, sensors.

02
Clean - Thorough Degreasing

Thermal cleaning (cast iron), shot blasting (external), ultrasonic cleaning (oil galleries), chemical degreasing (aluminium).

03
Inspect - Measure Everything

Cylinder bore wear, crankshaft journal wear, camshaft lobe wear, valve guide wear, block flatness, crack detection.

04
Machine - Restore to Spec

Cylinders honed/bored, crankshaft journals ground, cylinder head skimmed, valve seats cut, valve guides replaced.

05
Rebuild - Replace & Reassemble

New piston rings, main bearings, big-end bearings, thrust washers, timing chain/belt, oil pump, water pump, gaskets, seals, core plugs.

06
Test - Bench Test and Run

Oil pressure checked, compression tested, leak-down test, engine run (cold & hot) to check for leaks, noises, and correct operation.

"A proper reconditioning process takes time. When we strip an engine, we find things that weren't obvious from the outside - cracks, worn bearings, damaged oil galleries. That's why we never skip the inspection step. It's what separates a real reconditioned engine from a 'cleaned up' used engine."

Warranty - What You Get and What You Don't

Understanding warranty terms is essential before committing to any engine purchase.

Warranty Length Typical Coverage Typical Exclusions Best For

12 months Parts & labour (internal mechanical failure) Ancillaries, gaskets, seals, consequential damage Most reconditioned engine buyers
24 months Parts & labour (internal mechanical failure) Ancillaries, gaskets, seals, consequential damage Higher-value vehicles, long-term ownership

[EM-OBSERVED] based on supplier warranty terms from 17,365 quotes.

What the Warranty Covers (Typically)

  • Internal mechanical failure of the engine assembly (block, cylinder head, crankshaft, pistons, connecting rods, camshaft, valves, timing components).
  • Parts and labour (for the covered failure).

What the Warranty Does NOT Cover (Typically)

  • Ancillaries (alternator, starter, air conditioning compressor, turbocharger, injectors, fuel pump) - unless specifically stated.
  • Belts and hoses; gaskets and seals (if they fail after installation).
  • Damage caused by incorrect fitting, inadequate lubrication, or overheating after installation.
  • Damage caused by neglect or failure to maintain.
  • Consequential damage (e.g., if a component failure causes damage to other parts).

The Golden Rule: Always read the warranty terms before committing. A warranty is only as good as the supplier standing behind it. If a supplier cannot tell you the claims process, the exclusions, and the duration, they are not offering a real warranty.

Questions to Ask About Warranty:

  1. What exactly does the warranty cover? (Parts only; Parts and labour-)
  2. What is the duration- (6 months; 12 months; 24 months-)
  3. What is the claims process- (Who do I contact- What documentation do I need-)
  4. What are the exclusions- (Gaskets- Seals- Ancillaries- Consequential damage-)
  5. Is the warranty transferable if I sell the vehicle-
  6. What happens if I have a problem 6 months and 1 day after installation-

Reconditioned Engine Risks & Caveats

Reconditioned engines are the most popular choice - but they are not without risks.

Risk 1: Quality Varies by Supplier

Some suppliers cut corners - skipping machining, using cheap parts, or rushing the rebuild. Mitigation: Choose a supplier with clear documentation, photos of the rebuild, and 4.0+ star reviews.

Risk 2: "Reconditioned" vs "Rebuilt" Confusion

Terms are used interchangeably in the UK. Mitigation: Always ask for a detailed written specification of exactly what work will be performed.

Risk 3: Warranty Exclusions

Labour may not be covered, ancillaries excluded, consequential damage not included. Mitigation: Read the warranty terms carefully. Ask specifically about labour coverage and consequential damage.

Risk 4: Fitment Quality

Poor fitting can cause oil leaks, coolant leaks, premature wear, or immediate failure. Mitigation: Use a reputable garage with experience in your engine type. Ask the supplier for fitting recommendations.

Risk 5: Vehicle Value Mismatch

Don't spend £4,000 on a reconditioned engine if the car is only worth £2,500. Mitigation: Use the vehicle value rule in Section 7. Consider a used engine or scrapping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about buying a reconditioned engine in the UK.

How much does a reconditioned engine cost in the UK?
Based on 17,365 reconditioned engine quote requests in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reconditioned engines cost £2,100–£3,500 supply only or £3,500–£5,500 fitted. Prices vary by engine type, vehicle segment, and regional labour rates.
What's the difference between reconditioned and rebuilt?
A reconditioned engine has been disassembled, cleaned, inspected, and had worn or out-of-spec parts replaced (typically bearings, rings, seals, gaskets) with surfaces machined where necessary. A rebuilt engine is completely stripped and every wearable component is replaced regardless of condition, often with upgraded aftermarket parts for performance or known factory defect correction. Remanufactured engines go further still, returning the engine to original factory tolerances and OEM specifications.
What warranty comes with a reconditioned engine?
Most UK reconditioned engine suppliers offer 6 to 12-month warranties, with some premium suppliers offering up to 24 months. Standard warranties cover mechanical failure of the engine block, cylinder head, crankshaft, pistons, connecting rods, camshafts, and internal timing components. Warranties rarely cover external ancillaries (turbo, injectors, starter), gaskets/seals failure after installation, or consequential damage caused by overheating or oil starvation.
Is a reconditioned engine worth the extra cost over a used one?
For vehicles worth over £3,000 that you plan to keep for 2+ years, yes. A reconditioned engine costs roughly £500–£1,000 more than a used unit but delivers verified internal health, new wearing parts, documented machining, and a stronger warranty (6–12 months vs 1–6 months for used). For older, low-value vehicles (<£3,000) or short ownership horizons, a used engine may be more cost-effective.
How long does a reconditioned engine take?
Supply-only delivery takes 24–72 hours if the reconditioned engine is in stock on the shelf. If the unit needs to be built to order or your original core engine is reconditioned, turnaround is typically 3–7 working days. Supply and fit usually takes 5–10 working days total including collection, fitting, testing, and sign-off.
Can I fit a reconditioned engine myself?
While competent DIY mechanics can physically fit an engine, doing so usually restricts or voids the supplier's warranty to 'parts only' (excluding any labour claims). Professional garage installation provides a VAT invoice and ensures the warranty terms remain fully valid for both parts and labour.
What should I ask a reconditioned engine supplier before buying?
Always ask: 1) What exact machining was carried out on block and cylinder head? 2) Which parts were replaced with brand new items? 3) Does the warranty cover parts only or parts and labour? 4) Is core exchange required and who pays collection shipping? 5) Is VAT included in the quoted price? 6) What is your claim procedure if an issue occurs?
Do I need to tell the DVLA and my insurer?
Yes. If the engine number changes (which happens when replacing the block), you must notify the DVLA by updating section 7 of your V5C registration document, often requiring a receipt or garage letter confirming the new engine number and displacement. You must also notify your car insurance provider, even if the engine capacity and fuel type remain identical.
