Decision Framework

The Repair vs Replacement Decision Framework

The decision between repairing and replacing your engine comes down to three numbers: repair cost, replacement cost, and vehicle value. Use this framework to guide your thinking.

The Three-Question Test

1
Is the failure caught early?
(No bottom-end damage, no metal in oil)
✅ Repair may be possible
❌ Replace - damage is too extensive
2
Does repair cost exceed 50% of vehicle value?
❌ Replace - repair is uneconomical
✅ Repair may make financial sense
3
Is the vehicle worth more than the repair cost?
✅ Repair makes financial sense
❌ Replace or scrap - repair exceeds value

The Decision Flowchart

ScenarioRecommended Action
Minor failure caught early (timing chain rattle, head gasket seepage) ✅ Repair - £500-£1,500 is cheaper than £3,500+ for replacement
Advanced failure with bottom-end damage (rod knock, snapped timing chain on interference engine) ❌ Replace - Repair often costs more than replacement
Vehicle value under £4,000 and repair cost over £1,500 ❌ Replace or scrap - Repair is uneconomical relative to vehicle value
Vehicle value over £8,000 and repair cost under £2,000 ✅ Repair - Cost is well within vehicle value
Vehicle value over £8,000 and repair cost over £3,000 ⚠️ Evaluate both - Repair may be close to replacement cost
Classic or sentimental value ⚠️ Repair or rebuild - Value is personal, not market-based

When to Repair

When Repair Makes Sense

The "green light" criteria for repair - and common repairable failures.

The "Green Light" Criteria for Repair

🔔 Early failure detection
Symptoms caught before catastrophic damage - rattle, seepage, warning light
🔧 No bottom-end damage
No rod knock, no bearing failure, no metal in oil
💧 No contamination
Coolant not mixed with oil, no debris in oil system
📊 Low mileage on vehicle
Vehicle still has useful life - not at end of service life
💰 High vehicle value
Repair cost is a small percentage of vehicle value
✅ Known repairable failure
The failure is one of the common repairable types below
🚗 Vehicle otherwise sound
No rust, no other major mechanical issues across the body and transmission

When to Replace

When Replacement Is the Only Option

The "red light" criteria for replacement - and common replacement-only scenarios.

The "Red Light" Criteria for Replacement

⛓️ Snapped timing chain on interference engine
Valves have hit pistons - head and block damage
🔊 Rod knock or bearing failure
Bottom-end damage - crankshaft and bearings destroyed
⚙️ Metal shavings in oil
Internal wear has contaminated the entire oil system
🛑 Seized engine
Complete internal failure - engine is scrap
💔 Cracked block or cylinder head
Structural damage - cannot be repaired economically
📊 Repair cost exceeds 50% of vehicle value
Repair is uneconomical - money is better spent elsewhere
🔁 Multiple concurrent failures
Timing chain + turbo + injectors = repair cost exceeds replacement

Common Replacement-Only Scenarios

ScenarioWhy Replacement
Snapped timing chain (N47, EA189, etc.)Valves have hit pistons - head damage, possible block damage
Crankshaft bearing failure (SDV6, N57)Bearing has spun - crankshaft, block, and oil system contaminated
Complete seizurePistons have welded to cylinders - block is scrap
Cracked blockNo economic repair - replacement is the only option
Catastrophic overheatingWarped cylinder head, damaged block, oil degradation - multiple failures

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about engine repair

How do I know if my engine can be repaired? −
If the failure is caught early - before the engine has suffered catastrophic damage - repair is often possible. Signs include timing chain rattle, head gasket seepage, turbo whine, or injector issues. If you have rod knock, metal in oil, or a snapped timing chain, replacement is usually required.
How much does engine repair cost? +
Repair costs vary: Timing chain £650-£1,800, head gasket £800-£2,000, turbo £700-£2,500, injectors £400-£1,200, DPF £500-£1,500. Always get multiple quotes before committing.
Should I repair or replace my engine? +
Use the 50% rule: if repair cost exceeds 50% of your vehicle's value, replacement is usually better. If repair cost is under 30% of replacement cost, repair is usually better. Use the decision framework in Section 2.
What if my car is worth less than the repair cost? +
If repair cost exceeds vehicle value, seriously consider scrapping or replacing. Exceptions apply for classic cars, sentimental vehicles, or commercial vehicles where downtime is costly.
Can I repair a timing chain without replacing the engine? +
Yes - if caught early. A timing chain rattle caught before the chain snaps can be repaired for £650-£1,800. If the chain snaps, the engine is usually destroyed and replacement is required.
Is it worth repairing a head gasket? +
Yes - if caught before the engine overheats and warps the cylinder head. Head gasket repair costs £800-£2,000. If the head is warped, the cost increases significantly.
Should I repair my turbo or replace it? +
If the turbo has failed without sending debris into the oil system, repair is straightforward. Turbo replacement costs £700-£2,500. If debris has contaminated the oil system, the engine may also need work.
Do I need a diagnostic before deciding? +
Yes - diagnostics are essential to understand the full extent of the damage. Without diagnostics, you risk under-repairing (and having another failure) or over-repairing (spending more than necessary). Get a diagnostic quote →
