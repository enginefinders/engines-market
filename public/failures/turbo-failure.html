Which Repair Option Is Right For You?

Depending on the extent of internal damage, you have four main options — each offering distinct cost, turnaround, and warranty profiles.

  • Typical Cost: £195 – £600
  • Warranty: 3–12 months
  • Best For: Minor damage (seals, journal bearings, actuator)
  • When To Choose: Turbo housing intact, no shaft play beyond manufacturer tolerance, no debris in oil system.

  • Typical Cost: £495 – £1,200 + VAT
  • Warranty: 12–24 months

  • Typical Cost: £1,000 – £3,500+
  • Warranty: 12–24 months OEM

Signs of Turbo Failure

Don't Ignore These Turbo Warning Signs

A turbocharger rarely fails without prior warning. Listen to your engine's acoustic feedback and exhaust output to act early.

🔵 Early Warning (Act Soon · Save Money)
  • Loss of power / sluggish acceleration: Turbo isn't generating expected boost pressure under load.
  • Whistling or screaming noise: High-pitched acoustic whine that varies directly with throttle application.
  • Engine management light (EML): Common diagnostic fault codes P0299 (turbo underboost) and P0234 (overboost condition).
  • Increased fuel consumption: Engine management system over-fuelling to compensate for restricted boost air.
🔴 Progressed Damage (Investigate Urgently)
  • Blue smoke from exhaust: Internal turbo oil seals failed, leaking high-pressure oil directly into exhaust system.
  • Black smoke (over-fuelling): Insufficient charge air entering cylinders due to jammed vanes or seized compressor.
  • Rapid oil consumption: Turbo drawing engine oil into the induction tract and burning it during combustion.
  • Metallic grinding / rattling noise: Journal bearings completely worn; compressor/turbine wheel making physical contact with housing.
  • Excess oil pooling in intercooler: Heavy oil accumulation indicating total internal seal blowout.
🚨
STOP DRIVING IMMEDIATELY IF: You hear a severe metallic grinding/rattling noise OR the vehicle experiences sudden complete loss of boost accompanied by thick blue/black smoke. A disintegrating turbo compressor wheel will shatter and fling metal shrapnel through the intercooler, intake manifold, and directly into the cylinders — turning a £500 turbo replacement into a £3,000–£7,000 catastrophic engine write-off.

Preventative Engineering Protocols

5 Rules to Avoid Turbo Failure

A modern turbocharger operates at rotational speeds exceeding 150,000–250,000 RPM at temperatures over 850°C. Clean, uninhibited oil delivery is its fundamental lifeline.

01
Strict Oil Changes with Correct Manufacturer Spec
Turbo bearings rely entirely on hydrodynamic oil film. Extended service intervals degrade oil into abrasive carbon sludge. Always use exact OEM specs (e.g. BMW LL04, VW 507.00, Ford WSS-M2C913-D).
02
Cool-Down Idling After Motorway / Hard Driving
Allow 30–60 seconds of gentle driving or stationary idling before engine shutdown. Turning off immediately 'bakes' stationary oil inside scorching turbo bearings, creating carbon hotspots.
03
Use OEM-Quality Oil Filters & Clean Feed Pipes
Microscopic debris rapidly scores journal bearings. When replacing a turbo, always replace the oil feed pipe and clean/remove banjo bolt mesh filters (notably on Ford 1.6 TDCi).
04
Address Boost Leaks & Whistling Immediately
A leaking boost pipe forces the turbo to overspin to reach target manifold pressure, causing rapid thermal breakdown. A £60 boost hose repair prevents a £1,200 turbo blowout.
05
Periodic Turbo Chemical Decarbonisation on High-Mileage Diesels
Variable geometry turbo vanes (VNT) frequently clog with soot. Professional chemical cleaning frees stuck vanes before excessive strain strips the electronic actuator motor gears.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Turbo Failure

Clear answers to the most common questions on UK turbo repair costs, symptoms, and replacements.

How much does turbo replacement cost in the UK? +
Turbo repair/rebuild costs £195–£600. An exchange remanufactured turbo starts from £495+VAT (typically £600–£1,200 fully fitted with oil service). Brand new OEM turbos cost £1,000–£3,500+ fitted. Twin-turbo setups range from £1,500–£5,000+. Typical independent garage labour runs 3–8 hours at £65–£95/hour. View our full turbo replacement cost guide →
Can a turbo be repaired instead of replaced? +
Yes, depending on internal damage. Minor faults such as oil seal wear, electronic actuator failure, or initial journal bearing play can be rebuilt for £195–£600. However, exchange remanufactured turbos (£495+VAT) often represent better long-term value because they are precision-balanced on high-speed VSR flow rigs and backed by 12–24 month warranties.
What are the primary signs of turbo failure? +
The most common signs are sudden loss of power/limp mode, high-pitched whistling or siren noise under boost, thick blue or black exhaust smoke, engine management light with underboost/overboost DTC codes (P0299/P0234), rapid oil consumption, and metallic scraping sounds.
What causes turbocharger failure? +
The leading root causes are oil starvation (clogged feed pipes, missed oil services), oil contamination (carbon sludge or engine metal debris scoring bearings), foreign object damage (debris ingested through degraded air filters), carbonised VNT vanes sticking, and electronic actuator gear failure.
Can I drive with a failing turbo? +
If the turbo is merely whistling slightly or showing mild underboost, you should drive only directly to a repair specialist. If you hear metallic grinding, experience severe power drop, or see thick clouds of smoke, stop driving immediately. Continued operation risks compressor wheel disintegration, pulling metal shrapnel directly into the engine cylinders.
Is turbo failure covered under warranty? +
When purchasing a complete reconditioned engine, the turbocharger is typically included in the 12–24 month warranty term. However, failures stemming from external oil starvation, fuel dilution, or foreign debris are generally excluded. Standalone replacement turbos include 12–24 month manufacturer warranties. Engines Market is a comparison platform — we connect you with accredited UK suppliers. Read our warranty guide →
My turbo failed and sent metal fragments into the engine — what should I do? +
Do not crank or start the vehicle. You require a full mechanical inspection including borescope examination of cylinder walls. If metal debris has scored the cylinders or damaged valves, fitting a replacement reconditioned engine (£2,500–£7,000+) is required. The intercooler, charge pipes, and oil delivery circuits must also be completely replaced or ultrasonically flushed.
